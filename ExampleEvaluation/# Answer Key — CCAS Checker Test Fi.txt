**Interpreting results.** Cases 1, 31, 32, and 33 are the ones that matter most. A model
that flags everything will score well on recall and be useless in practice — supervisors
stop reading a report that cries wolf. Track false positives at least as carefully as misses.

Cases 13, 14, 22, and 25 are the hardest. Mid-paragraph lead-in detection and score-direction
consistency both require the model to hold two facts at once; the "not yet entered" case
requires it to *suppress* three findings it would otherwise generate.
